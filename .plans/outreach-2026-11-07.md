## Decisions
- **Sender:** Reeve's Gmail. Claude creates drafts only, after Reeve and Nathan approve the text here. They read every draft and press send themselves. Reason: outward actions need both yeses, and the address must belong to the name on it.
- **Reach:** every contact we could find with a public email, in Sacramento, California and nationally. Each gets its own personalized email, never a BCC blast. Reason: the community is wary of drive-by research; a personal note gets read.
- **The ask:** reply when they can; a short Zoom call if they're open to it, or leave a number and we call. No live try-it offer: we don't have a remote path yet (Nathan, 16:20, 16:50).
- **Consent:** anonymous by default. Names used only if they say so.
- **Demo:** a narrated video attached to the email, under 25 MB. Version 1 exists at `recordings/feelink-explainer.mp4`; Reeve and Nathan are remaking it (`.plans/video-handoff-2026-11-07.md`). Drafts are created only after the new video is ready. The email body carries the whole idea in plain text so a screen-reader user never has to open the video.
- **Nothing is claimed that hasn't happened.** The email says no blind person has tried it yet.

## Email (individuals)

Subject: Sac State Students would like your opinion on a phone tutoring tool for blind students

Hello {first name},

My name is Reeve. I'm writing to you {why}.

My teammate Nathan and I are students at Sacramento State. This weekend, at the Capital Hacks hackathon, we built a prototype called FeelInk: a tutoring tool that lets a blind student feel what a sighted tutor is drawing, through the vibration motor of the student's own Android phone.

Here is how it works. The tutor draws in a web browser on a laptop. The student rests one finger on their phone screen. As each stroke is drawn, the phone buzzes steadily while the finger is on the line, taps twice at a corner, taps three times at the end of a line, and goes quiet when the finger drifts off, so the finger learns to follow the shape. We are building it to teach shapes in geometry, such as a triangle, a square or a circle, as the tutor draws them; to trace a graph, such as a line rising on an axis or a curve; and to follow a line as a route, like the path from a classroom door to the stairs, so a student can practise directions before walking them. There is no app to install and no hardware to buy, just a web page in Chrome.

We built it because, as far as we can tell, when a teacher draws a diagram on the board, a blind student gets it in words, later as a tactile sheet, or not at all. We may be wrong about that. No blind person has tried FeelInk yet, and we present on Sunday morning. We would rather hear the truth from you than guess.

Attached is a short narrated video that shows how it works.

If you have two minutes, we would be grateful for a reply to any of these:
1. When a teacher or tutor drew something, how did you get it?
2. Would feeling the strokes through your phone help with shapes, graphs or directions, or is this the wrong problem?
3. What is the strongest reason it would not work?

If you would be open to a short Zoom call, reply with a time that works for you. Or leave your phone number and we will call you.

Anything you tell us stays anonymous unless you tell us we may use your name. If you would rather we not contact you again, say so and we won't.

Thank you,
Reeve and Nathan
Sacramento State, Capital Hacks 5.0
