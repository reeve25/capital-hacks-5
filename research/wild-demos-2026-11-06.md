## The 10

1. **Swarm Ears.** Someone is trapped after a collapse, and crews call for silence and listen, with too few listening devices. Neighbors open a web page and their phones sync into one listening array. Demo: a judge hides and taps; judges' own phones pin the spot on the big screen. Flag: open. Nearest, tagline only: TrioAcustico (thunder triangulation). Hardest: phone time sync and a loud hall. Lead: Sacramento CERT or Metro Fire.
2. **Catch.** A blind child can't play catch with a parent without a special beeping ball. A camera turns any ball into moving sound in the ears. Demo: a blindfolded judge stops a rolling ball by sound. Flag: open for playing. Spectator-only, taglines: MatchVision (UC Berkeley AI Hackathon, winner label), Buzzboard (HackGT 13). Hardest: end-to-end latency, likely needs wired earbuds. Lead: Society for the Blind, Sac State AT Lab.
3. **Flatline.** Someone using opioids alone has nobody there if breathing stops. A phone on a table tracks chest motion with inaudible sound, then calls for help. Demo: a judge holds their breath, the line goes flat, a phone rings. Flag: open at hackathons on mechanism; rivals there are wearables and dispatch (O.D. Guard, Drewbert, taglines). Tidal (Read, Oct 2026) reads breathing from a Ring camera for elders. UW's Second Chance did this in 2019 (Secondary), no release found. Hardest: consumer speakers and mics at high frequency; the subject is sensitive. Lead: local harm-reduction groups.
4. **Ghost Hands.** A family caregiver sees wound care or an injection once at discharge, then is alone at home. A nurse demonstrates once; translucent hands overlay your live hands and correct you. Demo: an untrained judge applies a tourniquet in a minute. Flag: partial. Taglines: Steady (Hack the Valley 11), LifeSaver (YHack Spring 2026), both surgical training with hand tracking and winner labels. Hardest: judging correctness, not just drawing hands. Lead: Sac State nursing.
5. **Mock Council.** A resident gets two minutes at public comment with no way to practice or know what moves a vote. Simulated members built from public records respond. Demo: a judge speaks; members reply and a vote meter moves. Flag: open. Don't clone real officials' voices, and label it a simulation. Hardest: proving the prediction, so backtest on past votes. Lead: Sac State government faculty, regular public commenters.
6. **Witness.** Someone hit in a crosswalk has only strangers' shaky clips, and syncing them takes experts. Phones sync on a clap and rebuild the moment in 3D. Demo: judges film a staged moment and scrub around it a minute later. Flag: open. Partial, tagline: HiveSight (TreeHacks 2026), multi-phone 2D map of people. Hardest: moving 3D reconstruction, likely rented GPU. Lead: legal aid, Sac State criminal justice.
7. **Motion X-Ray.** A bridge or building inspector can't see movement too small for the eye, and sensors cost too much for small towns. Phone video magnifies vibration. Demo: a judge sees their own pulse in their face, then a loosened bolt shifts a structure's frequency. Flag: mechanism exists. Taglines: Vibrovision, HumSight (OpenCV AI Competition 2026, machines), no winner labels. Tidal uses magnification too. RDI's commercial version is from memory, unread. Hardest: real diagnosis beyond a pretty video. Lead: Sac State civil engineering, Caltrans in Sacramento.
8. **Living Arrow.** In a crowd crush or smoke, people follow each other, not signs. Every phone in the crowd lights up as part of one moving arrow to the safest exit. Demo: judges scan a QR, phones ripple toward an exit, then reroute when it's blocked. Flag: partial. Crowd-crush monitoring is common; Eyrie (HackHarvard 2025, winner label, tagline). Phones-as-display guidance not found. Hardest: knowing where each phone is. Lead: Sac State emergency management.
9. **Mirror.** A stroke survivor watches a hand that won't move, and mirror boxes limit what therapy can do. A webcam shows the weak hand moving, driven by the good hand. Demo: a judge keeps one hand still and watches it move. Flag: partial. Phantom Mirror and thARapy (taglines, AR for phantom limb); stroke version not found. Hardest: convincing live segmentation, and evidence of benefit. Lead: Sac State physical therapy.
10. **Reality Diff.** A levee inspector walks the same miles and must remember whether a seep or crack grew. Film it twice; it aligns the walks and circles what changed. Demo: a teammate secretly moves something and the app finds it. Flag: crowded. Taglines: Delta (DubHacks '26, winner label), STRATA (OpenCV AI Competition). Hardest: alignment outdoors. Lead: California DWR in Sacramento.

## Near-exact rivals found, kept visible

- **Scam fire drill** (your voice cloned, then it calls your grandparent as a drill). Read: VoiceCircle and Mirage (ForgeHacks 2026, Oct 10, participation-style prizes only). Biggest gasp on the list and reuses our voice work, but near-exact.
- **Quake your room** (film a room, simulate the earthquake). Read: Quakeproof (Gemini 3 Hackathon, Feb 2026, no prize), video to Rapier physics plus fix suggestions.
- **Walk back in** (old photo to walkable 3D for dementia). Read: Living Photos (AI Builders Hackathon, Sep 2026, no prize). Taglines: Re:collection, aeon-v2, Deja View, Memory Palace.
- **Room pulse** (camera vitals in an ER waiting room). Read: VITAL (HackPrinceton Spring 2026, Best Healthcare Hack), which monitors waiting rooms with a vision model.
