# TalkBack and the FeelInk page (issue #91, Part A)

Sat Nov 7, 2026, written off-phone by the lead's research agent. Nothing here was measured on the Pixel. Labels: **Read** means a primary source read today (listed in Sources, cited as [S#]). **Reasoned** means inferred from what was read. **Measured** applies only to the probe test on a local headless Chromium. **Unknown** marks what Part B must find out. All code was read on its current main branch: AOSP frameworks/base, Chromium, and the open-source TalkBack snapshot of 2026-03-23 (commit 229212f). The Pixel runs Google's own TalkBack build and its installed Chrome, so Part B records both versions.

## Summary

1. **Most likely finding.** With TalkBack on, one-finger exploration of the canvas delivers no pointer or touch events to the page, so FeelInk stays silent. Android turns the finger into hover events, and Chrome hands those to its accessibility hit test instead of the page (Read [S5][S10][S11]). TalkBack speaks whatever accessibility node is under the finger, and the canvas has no name.
2. **Two fingers moving together** reach the page as one ordinary touch pointer at absolute screen coordinates. It is whichever finger is nearer its closest screen edge (Read [S5][S8]). The page then buzzes as if one finger were exploring, and phone-side drawing never starts.
3. **Two fingers moving apart** switch Android to "delegating", so both pointers reach the page as they are (Read [S5][S8]). In Live mode that starts a phone stroke on the tutor's board by accident (Reasoned from feel.html).
4. **A TalkBack double-tap** reaches the page as one synthetic pointerdown/mousedown/pointerup/mouseup/click. It has `pointerType` "mouse" and sits at the centre of the focused element: the screen centre for the full-screen canvas (Read [S12]).
5. **Our other gestures belong to TalkBack.** A two-finger tap pauses speech. A three-finger hold is TalkBack's screen search by default (Read [S9]). Fast one-finger traces are TalkBack gestures, and some of them are Back and Home (Read [S1]).
6. **A page cannot turn explore-by-touch off.** The pass-through region API exists only for accessibility services (Read [S3]). Chrome's `aria-touchpassthrough` origin trial (M92) has been removed from Chromium (Read [S13]). `role="application"` changes nothing for touch on TalkBack (Read [S14]; Reasoned).
7. **TalkBack has its own way out.** Its "pass through next gesture" (default: four-finger double-tap and hold) makes the next touch interaction, if it starts within 4 s, go straight to the page with every finger at its true position (Read [S1][S9]).
8. **Recommended first fix: a TalkBack mode the student opts into.** It treats any pointer as exploration and turns off phone drawing and multi-finger page gestures, so it works with the one-pointer drag, the two-pointer delegate and TalkBack's own pass-through. Add a named, instructed canvas (`aria-label` plus an announcement) that teaches two-finger exploring and TalkBack's pass-through gesture. Exit through Android Back, which works under TalkBack, not the three-finger hold.
9. **Detection.** No web API reveals TalkBack, and W3C design principle 2.11 says APIs must not reveal it (Read [S15]). The only strong page-side signal is the accessibility-click signature in point 4. So the student's opt-in is the primary switch, and the signature only offers the mode.
10. **Part B** (10 minutes, below) confirms or refutes points 1-5 and 7 on the Pixel with `node tools/talkback_probe.mjs`. The probe matched a known touch stream exactly on headless Chromium (Measured).

## 1. What the canvas most likely receives

### The pipeline (Read)
- **Touch exploration mode.** TalkBack requests `FLAG_REQUEST_TOUCH_EXPLORATION_MODE`, under which "a single finger moving on the screen behaves as a mouse pointer hovering over the user interface" [S2].
- **Multi-finger flags.** On Android 11 and later, TalkBack also sets `FLAG_REQUEST_MULTI_FINGER_GESTURES | FLAG_REQUEST_2_FINGER_PASSTHROUGH` [S7]. The second flag means "Two-finger swipe gestures are not detected, but instead passed through as one-finger gestures" (API 31) [S2].
- **Who classifies gestures on the Pixel.** On Android 13 and later, TalkBack's default (`values-v33`: `pref_talkback_gesture_detection_default` = true) is to classify touches itself through `TouchInteractionController` [S4][S7]. The class doing it is `TouchInteractionMonitor` [S8]. Each interaction ends up in one of these framework states [S4][S5]:
  - **Touch exploring:** one finger, after a double-tap-timeout delay. The framework converts the finger to `ACTION_HOVER_*` events [S4]. "One finger moving slow around performs touch exploration. One finger moving fast around performs gestures" [S5].
  - **Gesture:** consumed by TalkBack, and nothing reaches the app.
  - **Dragging:** two fingers that have each moved at least 3 × touch slop on a phone (`config_passthrough_slop_multiplier` = 3) [S8], in roughly the same direction. "Same direction" means the cosine between their movement vectors is at least 0.525, about 58° [S5][S6]. Only one pointer is passed to the app. It is chosen by `computeDraggingPointerIdIfNeeded`: "Use the pointer that is closest to its closest edge" [S5][S8]. Its coordinates are its real ones; the injected DOWN uses its original down position if exploration had not started yet [S5].
  - **Delegating:** two fingers moving in different directions, a drag that stops qualifying, or three fingers that all went down on the bottom edge. "Motion events will be passed through as-is" [S4][S5][S8]. Going from dragging to delegating sends an UP for the dragged pointer, then a DOWN for every finger [S5].
  - **Double-tap:** `performClick()`. The framework first tries `ACTION_CLICK` on the accessibility-focused node, then a simulated tap at its location [S4].
  - **Double-tap and hold:** `performLongClickAndStartDrag()`. A long press is simulated "on the last location with accessibility focus", and the finger's later movement is delegated [S4]. It is offset by (finger down position − focus click location), so motion is relative, not absolute [S6] (`EventDispatcher.longPressWithTouchEvents`).
  - **TalkBack pass-through:** TalkBack calls `setTouchExplorationPassthroughRegion` with the full screen and starts a 4 s guard timer [S9]. The next interaction stops the timer, and its end (`PASS_THROUGH_INTERACTION_END`) disables pass-through again [S9]. The framework sends interactions that start in that region "straight to the view hierarchy" [S3].
- **Chrome's side:**
  - `ContentView.onHoverEvent` forwards hovers to `EventForwarder.onHoverEvent`, which sends them to native code as mouse events with tool type finger [S10]. Natively, the parent handler `WebContentsViewAndroid::OnMouseEvent` gets them first, and "Hover events can be intercepted when in accessibility mode" [S11]. `WebContentsAccessibilityBase::OnHoverEvent` asks the Java side, which returns true whenever accessibility is enabled. It then runs an accessibility hit test, and the comment says "Hover event was consumed by accessibility by now. Return true to stop the event from proceeding" [S11]. So the renderer, and the page, never sees exploration hovers.
  - Ordinary touch events, including the injected drag and delegate streams, take `ContentView.onTouchEvent` to `EventForwarder.onTouchEvent`. That path has no accessibility branch; the only special case is a desktop-trackpad hack [S10].
  - An accessibility click goes through `AXObject::OnNativeClickAction`, which also calls `LocalFrame::NotifyUserActivation`, so `navigator.vibrate` is allowed afterwards [S12]. It continues to `AccessKeyAction(kFromAccessibility)` and then `DispatchSimulatedClick`. That fires pointerdown, mousedown, pointerup, mouseup and click [S12], with `pointerId` = the mouse id, `pointerType` "mouse", and clientX/Y at "the center of the" element's border box [S12].

### What feel.html does with each case (Reasoned from the pipeline and feel.html)

| Student does | Android state | Page receives | FeelInk today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 finger, slow, over the line | touch exploring: hover | nothing (Chrome's accessibility layer takes it) | silent. TalkBack speaks the node under the finger: the canvas is unnamed, so probably nothing useful, maybe the `#hint` text near the top (Unknown) |
| 1 finger, quick stroke | gesture | nothing | silent; TalkBack may act on the gesture (see table 2) |
| 2 fingers side by side, moving together | dragging | 1 touch pointer at the true position of the finger nearer an edge; pointerdown/move/up plus touchstart/move/end | buzzes along that finger: works as exploration. Phone pen never starts (only 1 pointer) |
| 2 fingers that drift apart or turn | delegating | up for the dragged pointer, then 2 pointers as-is | `fingers.size === 2` in Live, so a phone stroke starts and is posted to the tutor's board: accidental drawing |
| 1 finger resting, 2nd finger moving (today's "add a finger to draw") | the resting finger never reaches 3 × slop, so no decision | nothing new | no pen, no buzz; TalkBack may keep exploring with the first finger |
| double-tap anywhere | performClick on the focused node | synthetic pointerdown/up (`pointerType` mouse) + mousedown/up + click at the node's centre, no touch events | if the canvas holds focus: one feel at the screen centre (a buzz only if the line passes there); the Live sender posts a down and an up at the centre. If no node has focus, nothing (Unknown) |
| double-tap and hold, then slide | long click + delegated drag, offset to the focus location | 1 real touch pointer starting at the focused node's centre, moving with the finger | feels a copy of the finger's path shifted so it starts at the screen centre: wrong positions unless the finger started at the centre |
| 4-finger double-tap and hold, then touch within 4 s | pass-through region, full screen | that whole interaction raw: every finger, true positions | everything works as with TalkBack off, until the last finger lifts |
| 3 fingers held 1 s (Live: home) | gesture detector: 3-finger single-tap-and-hold | nothing (3 pointers are delegated only from the bottom edge) | no home; TalkBack opens screen search (default mapping [S9]) |

## 2. Our gestures against TalkBack's

TalkBack gestures below are the defaults from the help page [S1] and the open-source defaults file [S9]. Google's build may differ, and TalkBack lets users reassign most gestures, though "Two-finger swipe gestures can't be reassigned" [S1].

| FeelInk gesture (where) | What TalkBack does with it | Collision | Page gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-finger explore (Live, practice, test) | explore by touch: "Drag a finger across the screen to hear the item beneath it" [S1] | **total**: the core interaction never reaches the page | nothing |
| 1-finger fast trace of a shape | swipe right/left = next/previous item; angle gestures: up then left = **Home**, down then left = **Back**, left then up = Recents, right then down = Notifications [S1] | **high**: a fast L or square trace can leave Chrome (Reasoned: a trace has to match TalkBack's recognizer to trigger it) | nothing |
| 2-finger draw (Live: second finger held while the first moves) | two-finger drag = scroll, passed through as one finger [S1][S2] | **high**: drawing never starts as designed; diverging fingers draw by accident | 1 pointer, or 2 when delegated |
| 2-finger tap: next shape / end trial (practice, test) | 2-finger tap = pause or resume speech [S1][S9] | **total** | nothing |
| 2-finger hold 1 s: back (practice) | 2-finger tap-and-hold = `LONG_PRESS_TALKBACK_EXIT` [S9] (a mis-triggering recovery action; its exact behaviour outside the tutorial was not read) | **total**, effect Unknown | nothing |
| 3-finger hold 1 s: home (Live) | 3-finger tap = TalkBack menu; 3-finger single-tap-and-hold = screen search [S9] | **total** | nothing |
| 2-finger swipe started near a side or bottom edge | with gesture navigation: 2-finger swipe from a side edge = Back, from the bottom = Home [S1] | **medium** for drawing or exploring near the edges | Chrome may lose focus |
| 3-finger swipe up from the bottom | accessibility menu [S1]; delegated as-is from the bottom edge [S8] | low | system UI |
| Buttons and sliders on home, reveal and done | swipe to focus, double-tap to activate; slider: swipe up/down or the angle gestures [S1] | none: standard controls work | click (Read [S12]) |
| `speechSynthesis` landmark names | TalkBack speaks too | **medium**: two voices; `speechSynthesis.cancel()` in `say()` does not stop TalkBack (Reasoned) | n/a |

## 3. Candidate solutions

### A. TalkBack mode: any pointer explores, no phone drawing (recommended first fix)
- **What it is.** A setting the student turns on ("I use TalkBack") that, while on:
  - feels the first pointer down and ignores the others;
  - never creates `pen`, and turns off the 2-finger tap, 2-finger hold and 3-finger hold page gestures;
  - exits Live through Android Back, which the phone-product issue already plans ("Android Back exits Live", STATUS 16:16) and which TalkBack users reach by down-then-left or a two-finger edge swipe [S1].
- **Why it is first.** It handles every stream the platform can deliver (Reasoned): one pointer from a two-finger drag, two from a delegate, raw touches from TalkBack's pass-through, and the centre click from a double-tap. And it removes the accidental-drawing hazard.
- **Trade-offs:**
  - The student explores with two fingers together, which is unusual.
  - The felt point is under the finger nearer its closest screen edge, not necessarily the one the student thinks of as leading. Side-by-side fingertips are roughly 15-20 mm apart (Reasoned, not measured) against a 64.6 mm wide screen and a 1-5 mm strong core, so which finger is felt matters. The instructions must say "the finger nearer the phone's edge".
  - Fingers that drift apart switch to delegating mid-stroke. In this mode that only changes which pointer is felt, so the stroke stays smooth if the first pointer is kept.
  - Phone-side drawing is lost for TalkBack users. Drawing is the tutor's job in Reeve's 16:16 brief ("the tutor controls the lesson, the student controls exploration"), so this costs little.
  - Small code change in feel.html: a flag consulted in `pointerdown`, `release` and `checkGestures`, plus a settings toggle.

### B. TalkBack's own pass-through gesture (keep TalkBack, get raw touch)
- **What it is.** The student does a four-finger double-tap and hold, hears the chime, and puts a finger down within 4 s. That whole touch interaction then goes to the page untouched [S1][S9]. One-finger exploring, absolute positions and the current multi-finger gestures all work until the last finger lifts.
- **Trade-offs:**
  - A four-finger double-tap and hold on a 6.2 in phone is awkward.
  - The first use shows an education dialog (`PassThroughModeDialog`) [S9].
  - It lasts one interaction, so every lift means doing it again. Exploring a shape with lifts gets slow.
  - It is TalkBack's documented feature, not ours, so it respects Reeve's "rather than silently disabling accessibility features".
  - Works only on Android 11 and later (`supportPassthrough()` = R+ [S7]); the Pixel 8 qualifies.
  - Whether Google's build keeps the same default gesture is Unknown until Part B.
- **Teach both.** A plus B is the strongest combination: A makes the page correct whatever arrives, and B gives full-precision one-finger exploring when the student wants it.

### C. `role="application"`, a name and spoken instructions
- **What it does for touch.** The spec says assistive technologies that intercept standard input events "SHOULD switch to a mode that passes most or all standard input events through to the web application" [S14]. TalkBack's only Chrome-role handling found in its source is keyboard browse mode for `listBox` and `menuBar` [S14]. Explore-by-touch is decided in the framework and TalkBack before Chrome sees a role [S5][S8]. So it will not pass touches through (Reasoned; Part B can confirm by hearing the announcement and reading the probe).
- **What it is worth.** As an announcement channel it helps: give the canvas `role="application"` (or `img`) with `aria-label="FeelInk drawing"` and `aria-describedby` instructions. Then exploring onto it says what to do: "Explore with two fingers together, or four-finger double-tap and hold for one-finger mode". An `aria-live="polite"` status can confirm "TalkBack mode on".
- **Trade-offs:** almost free. `role="application"` requires a name [S14]. Its desktop meaning (forms mode) is irrelevant here, so `role="img"` with a label may read more naturally (Reasoned).
- **A conflict to settle.** The spoken landmark names (`say()`) would talk over TalkBack. In TalkBack mode, an `aria-live` region for landmark names lets TalkBack's own voice say them instead (Reasoned).

### D. An "Explore" control that TalkBack users double-tap
- **What the platform allows.** A web page cannot ask Android for pass-through. `setTouchExplorationPassthroughRegion` is an `AccessibilityService` method [S3]. Chrome's `aria-touchpassthrough` ran as an origin trial from M92 and is gone from current Chromium: `kTouchPassthroughDeprecated` in `ax_enums.mojom`, no `touch_passthrough_manager` on main, and the ARIA issue is still open with "no consensus on the syntax" [S13].
- **So the control can only:**
  - (1) switch the page into mode A;
  - (2) speak option B's instructions;
  - (3) act as a large target for double-tap-and-hold, so the delegated drag starts at its centre [S4][S6].

  Variant 3 gives relative, not absolute, motion. If the control is full-screen and the student starts the hold at the screen centre, positions are nearly right; otherwise everything is shifted (Reasoned).
- **Trade-offs:** (1) and (2) are cheap and worth having as the visible entry to A. (3) is unreliable for a shape-reading product and should not be the main path.

### E. Native wrapper (out of scope this weekend)
- **What it is.** One of two native builds:
  - A small Android app hosting the page in a WebView. Its own `View.OnHoverListener` would read the explore-by-touch hover coordinates (touch exploration makes the finger "a mouse pointer hovering" [S2]) and pass them into the page.
  - A companion accessibility service that sets a pass-through region over the canvas [S3], the way TalkBack's braille keyboard does [S9].
- **Trade-offs:** one-finger exploring with TalkBack fully on. But it is an install and a second accessibility service, both big asks of a blind student, and Play policy review. Days, not hours. It belongs in the pitch's "what's next", not in Sunday's build.

### F. Turning TalkBack off for the session
It works, but Reeve's brief rules it out as a default ("rather than silently disabling accessibility features"). It stays only as the student's own informed choice, never something the page does or nudges silently.

## 4. Detecting TalkBack from a page

There is no direct API. Chrome exposes no touch-exploration state to the web (Reasoned from the Chrome sources read; no such binding was found). The W3C principle is explicit: "Make sure that your API doesn't provide a way for authors to detect that a user is using assistive technology" (§2.11, which notes the Accessibility Object Model dropped such events) [S15]. Heuristics, strongest first:

| Heuristic | How | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| Student opt-in toggle | "I use TalkBack" in settings, remembered in localStorage | **reliable and consented**; the primary switch |
| Accessibility-click signature | pointerdown with `pointerType` "mouse" plus a click at the element's exact centre, with no touchstart before them, on a phone with no mouse [S12] | **high precision, low coverage**: fires only when the user double-taps something. Other services that click through accessibility actions (for example Switch Access or Voice Access, Reasoned) look the same, which is still "assistive technology present" |
| Instructions control reached | a visually hidden "How to explore" button first in the DOM, reached only by swiping through elements | medium: a by-product of offering instructions; sighted keyboard users can reach it too |
| Silence while the page is live | no pointer events for a long time while Live is open | low: identical to the student not touching |
| Pointer-stream shape | a lone pointer whose pointerdown arrives late (after 3 × slop of movement), with a second finger never reported | low: hard to tell from a normal late touch; not worth coding |
| Lead-side adb check | `adb shell settings get secure touch_exploration_enabled` (read-only [S16]) on the demo rig, pushed to the page through the relay | reliable **only on our USB rig**, not a product feature |
| Media queries | `prefers-reduced-motion`, `forced-colors` | unrelated to TalkBack; do not use |

Recommendation: the opt-in toggle switches mode A. The click signature only offers it once, out loud ("Screen reader detected? Turn on TalkBack mode in settings"). Detection stays on the phone: never log it, never send it to the relay or stage, and never change behaviour without telling the student (Reasoned from [S15]).

## 6. The probe (`tools/talkback_probe.mjs`)

- **What it does.** Node 22 with no dependencies. It reads `http://127.0.0.1:9222/json` and attaches only to a page whose URL is on `http://localhost:8765`, preferring `/feel`. It never prints another tab's URL. It adds passive capture listeners on `window` for pointerdown/move/up/cancel, touchstart/move/end/cancel and click. Passive listeners cannot cancel events, so they cannot change the page's behaviour. After N seconds (default 20) it prints a table and removes the listeners. If the page reloaded during the window, it says the counts were lost instead of printing zeros.
- **Options.** `--devtools` and `--origin` accept only loopback URLs and exist for testing. `--selftest` checks the counting, the clean-up, the tab picking and the argument guard offline.
- **Measured, headless Chromium, not the phone.**
  - Setup: Playwright's chromium-headless-shell build 1248, DevTools on 127.0.0.1:9333, feel.html served from 127.0.0.1:8799, Live mode clicked. Free RAM was 1.85 GB before the start.
  - Injected through CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`: one finger (down, 10 moves, up), then two fingers (down, second down, 5 moves each, both up).
  - The probe printed: pointerdown 3, pointermove 20, pointerup 3, touchstart 3, touchmove 15, touchend 3, click 0, 3 distinct pointers, max 2 down, max 2 touches, `touch` 26. That is exactly the injected stream.
  - A second run added a mouse hover and a mouse click at (206, 457) on the 412 × 915 viewport. It counted 1 hover pointermove, a `mouse` pointerdown/up, 1 click, and 1 pointerdown + 1 click at the page centre, as designed.
  - Afterwards `'__talkbackProbe' in window` was false.
  - Against the same browser with the default origin it refused: "no tab on http://localhost:8765 (1 other tabs not shown)".
- **Not run.** The probe has not touched the Pixel. It is not wired into `tools/check.sh`; `node tools/talkback_probe.mjs --selftest` is the runnable check.

- [S9] TalkBack `PassThroughModeActor.java`, `Mappers.java`, `res/values/donottranslate.xml` (same commit)

  - Defaults: `4finger_2tap_hold` = pass through next gesture, `2finger_1tap` = pause/resume feedback, `3finger_1tap_hold` = screen search, `2finger_1tap_hold` = `LONG_PRESS_TALKBACK_EXIT`.
