**3. Toughest judge question**

**Reasoned:** “CUFF already guides the cane arm, and PossessedHand already points an electrically moved hand toward a destination. What benefit requires moving the very wrist responsible for protective cane sweeping?” [CUFF](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/barontini_et_al_final_submission.pdf), [PossessedHand](https://www.interaction-ipsj.org/archives/paper2010/short/0188/0188.pdf).

**Reasoned — Strongest honest answer:** “The proposed distinction is preserving the traveler’s own unchanged cane and practiced sweep while adding route guidance. We have not demonstrated an advantage or noninterference. Our prototype must show readable direction cues, maintained sweep coverage and immediate voluntary override; otherwise the central claim fails.” [O&M coverage requirements](https://ableaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Deafblind-Orientation-and-Mobility-Best_Practice_Principles-Leaflet-Updated-20-11-2020.pdf).

**4. Concept critique**

- **Read:** O&M guidance specifies a cane arc centred on the body, extending beyond both shoulders and coordinated with opposite-foot steps. Constant-contact technique retains that arc and rhythm. An individualized COMS plan explicitly describes wrist-generated sweeping. [Able Australia guide, printed p.5](https://ableaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Deafblind-Orientation-and-Mobility-Best_Practice_Principles-Leaflet-Updated-20-11-2020.pdf), [COMS plan](https://www.focusonvisionandvisionloss.org/uploads/5/5/4/9/55499297/mobility_plan-long_cane.pdf).

- **Reasoned:** This is a genuine control conflict, **not automatically fatal**. A constant steering torque alternately assists and opposes the oscillating wrist. It could change sweep width, timing or ground contact; cane-tip impacts also change the load felt at the wrist. A successful stationary-hand demo would not settle compatibility. [Technique basis](https://www.focusonvisionandvisionloss.org/uploads/5/5/4/9/55499297/mobility_plan-long_cane.pdf).

- **Reasoned:** Slowly shifting the sweep’s centre could convey direction across several sweeps. But until the torso and walking path turn too, that shift reduces coverage on one side of the actual travel corridor. Wrist movement does not guarantee corresponding foot placement. Phase-aware, overridable guidance is a plausible hypothesis requiring demonstration. [Centred-sweep rationale](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsmbe/Annual59/Proc/Annual59_823/_pdf/-char/ja).

- **Read:** CUFF users sometimes confused its return movement with another turn cue; experienced cane users slowed to interpret guidance and preferred choosing their own avoidance strategy. “Physical” therefore does not establish “effortless” or “intuitive.” [CUFF discussion](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/barontini_et_al_final_submission.pdf).

- **Read → Reasoned:** Forearm EMS can unintentionally move adjacent fingers/wrist, and palm rotation changes electrode–muscle alignment. Preserving cane grip and predictable actuation is consequently a substantive implementation problem. [CHI 2021 primary paper](https://lab.plopes.org/published/2021-CHI-BackHandEMS.pdf).

- **Reasoned — Likely first user/instructor objection, not an interview finding:** “Can I still feel the ground, clear my next step, and instantly reject a wrong instruction?” Skin stretch or squeeze may interfere less with joint motion, but then the demonstrated claim is a tactile instruction rather than mechanically steering the wrist—and CUFF is already close prior work. [O&M requirements](https://ableaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Deafblind-Orientation-and-Mobility-Best_Practice_Principles-Leaflet-Updated-20-11-2020.pdf), [CUFF](https://www.centropiaggio.unipi.it/sites/default/files/barontini_et_al_final_submission.pdf).
